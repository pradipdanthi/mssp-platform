What the internet can already see

Every company has the website it remembers — and three it forgot.

28 October 2026 · 3 min read · Kevantic Cyber Security

Ask a leadership team how many websites the company has, and they will name the main one. Then there is the campaign page from two years ago, the VPN hostname left up for a contractor, the certificate that expired on a name nobody owns in the current org chart, and the test shop a developer pointed at a real domain over a weekend. Attackers do not need your directory. They need the list the internet already published.

What belongs on that list

  • Domain names the company still answers for, including the ones marketing spun up.
  • Certificates that are expired, mismatched, or about to lapse.
  • Remote access, admin pages, and file services that respond from outside.
  • Names that point at an old host nobody patched because nobody remembers it.

A finding is a decision, not a trophy

Kevantic’s external attack surface service keeps that perimeter list current. A finding earns its place when it has an owner and one of three decisions: close it, fix it, or accept it in writing. An unread export is not discovery.

Use the list to drive which weakness to fix first. The internet-facing item outranks the quiet one every time.

How forgotten names appear

A product launch creates a domain. The campaign ends. The DNS record stays, pointing at a small server somebody stood up in a hurry. A contractor’s remote access was opened for a plant shutdown and never closed. A certificate warning has been clicking past for months because “it still works.” None of these feel like security projects. All of them are how an outsider draws a map without ever phishing your staff.

Kevantic’s external attack surface service keeps that map current: domains, certificates, and services that answer from outside. The Cloud SOC does not need you to admire the map. It needs an owner on each line. Close it, fix it, or accept it in writing until a date. Pair the line that is both exposed and fragile with NikTiar Aegis, so the patch work follows the exposure instead of the other way around.

Bring whoever buys domains into the same review as whoever runs the firewall. They are often different people, and the gap between them is where the forgotten name lives.

Once a quarter, sit with whoever buys domains and whoever runs DNS. Read the list out loud. The names nobody recognises are the work.
