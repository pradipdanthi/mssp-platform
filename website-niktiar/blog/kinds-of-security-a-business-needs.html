The kinds of security a business actually needs

When a board asks whether the company is secure, the useful answer is a set of jobs with owners — not a product name.

28 October 2026 · 3 min read · Kevantic Cyber Security

A director asks a fair question in a budget meeting: “Are we secure?” A single product name is a poor answer. Security that holds up on a Tuesday morning is a set of jobs. If one job has no owner, the others can look busy and still fail.

Know the doors

Most damaging intrusions do not start as a movie-style break-in. They start with a real username, a remote-access page left on the internet, or a system nobody meant to publish. The first jobs are to watch sign-in, to put a dedicated control on the edge, and to look at the company the way a stranger already can.

That is why stolen logins, edge devices, and what the internet can already see are separate articles. Cloud and Identity Protection watches the account. NikTiar SecureEdge sits on an edge dedicated to that customer. External attack-surface discovery lists the names and certificates an outsider can already reach.

Watch what happens after someone is inside

A login that succeeds is the start of the story. The useful question is what that account does next: a new mailbox rule, a second stolen password, a server talking to a server it never talks to, a laptop that starts encrypting files. Those are three more jobs — detection inside the network, a record that still exists next month, and a human case with an owner.

NikTiar DeepSight NDR watches movement inside the network. Log and Event Monitoring keeps the record. Incident Response turns a signal into a case with an owner, rather than a row nobody opens. Seeing movement inside the network and the hour after a phishing click go into those jobs.

Be able to show the proof

Boards and customers in healthcare, payments, and other regulated work ask whether technical safeguards are actually on. Continuous compliance scorecards cover CIS, ISO 27001, PCI-DSS, HIPAA §164.312, and NIST as indicators of those safeguards. They are not a certificate. The scorecard stays honest when the raw logs that support it remain on the NikTiar Edge Node, in the customer’s own environment.

Pick the door that is most exposed, and put a name on the person who watches it. Kevantic’s 24/7 Cloud SOC is that owner for the jobs you hand us. One hundred percent of raw logs stay on the NikTiar Edge Node.

If you only read two more pages, read the one that matches your most exposed door, then open the service map and see which tier covers it.
