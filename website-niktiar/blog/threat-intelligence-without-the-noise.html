Threat intelligence without the noise

Four hundred indicators in an inbox is a hobby. One match against your file server is intelligence.

28 October 2026 · 3 min read · Kevantic Cyber Security

Threat intelligence has a reputation problem. It arrives as a long list of addresses and file names, most of which have nothing to do with the company reading them. People archive the mail and feel briefly informed. Nothing in the building has been checked.

The only indicator that matters today

An indicator matters when it matches something you run, on a day you can still do something about it. A match against a laptop that called out last Tuesday is a case. A match against a campaign that never touched your industry is reading material. Sorting those two apart is the service.

Look backward, not only forward

New indicators are published after some victims have already been touched. Kevantic threat intelligence includes retrospective sweeps across retained telemetry — about 90 days on this service — so a host that spoke to an address last month becomes a case, not a surprise in next quarter’s report. That sweep is only as long as the logs you kept on the NikTiar Edge Node.

The customer summary names the business system involved. The raw event stays in the operational record. Pair a match with what the network shows before you decide the finding was a one-off.

How to read a finding from us

A Kevantic intelligence finding should name a system you recognise, a time, and a recommended next step a manager can approve. “An address associated with a current campaign was contacted by the jump host on 12 October; the Cloud SOC has a case open” is intelligence. A forwarded list of four hundred unrelated names is not. Retrospective sweeps look back across about 90 days of telemetry kept for this service, which is why log retention and intelligence are the same conversation.

Use the finding to decide scope, not to collect trivia. If the host that matched also walked toward a file server, read the network article next. If the account that owned the host has been signing in from somewhere odd, read the identity article. Intelligence that never touches those neighbouring jobs becomes a newsletter.

You can ignore industry news that does not match your environment. You should not ignore a match against a machine you still operate. That bias — toward your environment, away from the global firehose — is the point of the service.

Unsubscribe, mentally, from any feed that cannot answer: did this touch us, and when? If it cannot, it is news, not protection.
