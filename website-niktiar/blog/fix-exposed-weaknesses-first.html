Fix the exposed weakness before the quiet one

A monthly patch meeting cannot keep pace with a clock that now runs in hours.

28 October 2026 · 3 min read · Kevantic Cyber Security

A weakness is not urgent because a scanner printed a large number next to it. It is urgent when the system is reachable, the weakness is already being used against organisations like yours, and the system matters to the business. Everything else can wait for a planned window. The reachable items cannot.

Microsoft’s 2026 Digital Defense Report projects about 72,000 publicly disclosed vulnerabilities in 2026, and says the time from a flaw being seen in the wild to active use can be under 24 hours. A quarterly change board does not move that fast. A list sorted only by score spends those hours on the wrong machine.

A better order of work

  • What can a stranger on the internet already touch?
  • Is that weakness being used this week, or only theoretically possible?
  • If it is misused, does the business stop, or does a test server no one relies on stop?
  • Who can schedule the change, and who accepts the risk in writing if it waits?

How NikTiar Aegis fits

NikTiar Aegis is Kevantic’s vulnerability management service. It prioritises what is exposed and reachable, then gives patch guidance operations can actually schedule. It belongs next to external attack surface, so you are not patching an internal server the internet cannot see while a forgotten test site sits open.

What a useful weekly list looks like

Ten items, not two hundred. Each item names the system in business language (“the VPN appliance,” “the old campaign site”), says whether a stranger can reach it, says whether that class of weakness is being used right now, and names the person who will book the change. A spreadsheet of scores without those four fields is a library. NikTiar Aegis is meant to produce the short list, with patch guidance operations can schedule.

Do not “fix” a weakness by hiding it behind a hope that nobody will look. If a service must stay on the internet, the weakness needs a real change or a written acceptance with an expiry date. If it does not need to be on the internet, removing it beats patching it. External attack surface is how you notice the second case, especially the systems nobody put on the asset list.

The clock is the reason this cannot wait for a quarterly workshop. When a weakness is already being used, the organisations that lose the week are usually the ones still sorting the list by severity score alone.

If two weaknesses land on the same morning, fix the one a stranger can already reach. Keep the score. Change the order.
