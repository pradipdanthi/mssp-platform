Compliance scorecards without shipping every log

A score painted on a slide is not a control. A score that moves when encryption drops is closer.

28 October 2026 · 3 min read · Kevantic Cyber Security

A score of 78 on a slide does not encrypt a laptop. A score that falls when disk encryption is off, or when audit logging is disabled, is closer to the truth. Leaders need the second kind, because the first kind fails the moment someone asks a follow-up question.

What the five names actually mean here

Kevantic continuous compliance shows live indicators for CIS, ISO 27001, PCI-DSS, HIPAA §164.312, and NIST. Read them as technical-safeguard indicators: is the control present and behaving. They are not a claim that Kevantic certifies your company, and they are not a substitute for your own audit.

HIPAA §164.312, in this scorecard, points at safeguards such as encryption, audit logging, file integrity, and the ability to contain a host. It is an indicator of those safeguards. It is not a certification badge.

The log question sits underneath the score

For a clinic, or anyone whose logs might contain health information, the design choice matters as much as the number. Raw logs stay on the NikTiar Edge Node. The customer portal shows the scorecard and the case. Seeing the score does not require shipping the raw log off the network. That split is explained in where security logs should live.

The service itself is Continuous Compliance and Hardening.

How a director should read the portal

Look for movement, not a trophy number. Did endpoint encryption drop on a group of laptops? Did audit logging stop on a server that handles payments or care? Did a containment control that you believe is armed fail to acknowledge? Those are the moments a scorecard earns its place. Kevantic shows CIS, ISO 27001, PCI-DSS, HIPAA §164.312, and NIST as technical-safeguard indicators in the customer portal. They describe controls. They do not certify the company.

Then ask the residency question in the same breath. If the only way to see the score is to copy raw logs, including logs that may contain health information, out of your environment, the scorecard has created a new risk while describing an old one. On NikTiar, the raw logs stay on the Edge Node. The portal shows the indicator and the case.

Bring the scorecard to the same meeting as operations. A control that fails and nobody can name the owner will fail again next month, at a prettier percentage.

If a provider’s compliance view only works after every raw log has left your building, ask what else left with it.
