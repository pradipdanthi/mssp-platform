Where your security logs should live

There is a quiet second bill hiding in many security contracts: the copy of your own logs.

28 October 2026 · 3 min read · Kevantic Cyber Security

A common security contract has a quiet shape. To be watched, you copy every log to someone else’s cloud. Then you pay again to keep the copy. For a clinic, a payment environment, or a plant whose logs contain process detail, that copy is the real decision. It is made on page one and felt for years.

What “kept local” has to mean

Kept local means the raw record is still in the customer’s environment if the contract ends on a Thursday. It means an investigator can ask what a host did last month without first exporting every system. It means sensitive records are not duplicated into a place the customer does not operate, just so a dashboard can paint a chart.

Where Kevantic keeps the record

The NikTiar Edge Node sits in the customer environment and keeps 100 percent of the raw logs there. Log and Event Monitoring is the service built on that fact. The 24/7 Cloud SOC receives what it needs to detect and respond. You are not billed a second time for the privilege of storing your own history. That is what zero cloud log tax means on this site.

The same choice supports compliance scorecards and evidence after an alert. A scorecard that required the raw log to leave would break the promise the scorecard is trying to make.

What the Cloud SOC still needs to see

Keeping raw logs local is not the same as hiding the company from its own defenders. The Cloud SOC still needs the signal required to detect, investigate, and respond: the alert, the context, the ability to ask for the record that sits on the Edge Node. What you should refuse is the model that copies everything, stores it far away, and then charges a second fee so you can search your own history.

That distinction matters most where the logs are sensitive. A clinic should not have to export raw records that may contain health information just to be monitored. A payment environment should not have to duplicate cardholder-adjacent logs into a platform it does not operate. A plant should not have to ship process history off site to learn that a PC is misbehaving.

Put the question in the contract review, not in the incident. Thursday is a bad day to discover the logs live only in a tenancy you are about to leave.

Ask any provider, including us: if we part ways on a Thursday, where are the logs on Friday, and who can still read them?
