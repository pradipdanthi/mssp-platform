How to choose a managed security provider

A feature grid hides the five answers that decide whether the service will hold.

28 October 2026 · 3 min read · Kevantic Cyber Security

Proposals for managed security often look identical by page three. Everyone watches, everyone responds, everyone has a portal. The differences that matter are operational, and they fit in five questions. Ask them of every provider on the short list, including Kevantic.

Five questions

  1. Where do the raw logs live, and who can still read them if the contract ends?
  2. When you isolate a computer, what proves it actually isolated, rather than a ticket that says it did?
  3. Who is working at 2 a.m., and can they write a summary a director can forward without a translator?
  4. Do you watch what a valid login does next, or only whether the password was right?
  5. Can you show the internet-facing names and devices we did not know we had?

How Kevantic answers them

Raw logs stay on the NikTiar Edge Node in your environment. A computer is marked isolated only after the endpoint confirms it, not because a ticket says so. A 24/7 Cloud SOC owns the case and writes a summary a director can forward. Cloud and Identity Protection watches Microsoft Entra ID and Active Directory after a sign-in succeeds. External attack surface is the list of names and services already published on the internet. The customer view is on the platform page. The jobs behind it are in the kinds of security a business actually needs.

Kevantic Cyber Security Solutions is based in Bengaluru. The work is a managed service: the Cloud SOC and the NikTiar platform, not a software licence you are left to run alone. If a provider cannot answer the five questions in plain language, the brochure is doing more work than the service.

What to bring to the first conversation

You do not need a finished architecture diagram. Bring an honest sketch: how many people and sites, whether you run a plant, a clinic, or ordinary offices, which logins matter (email, VPN, finance), and whether raw logs are allowed to leave the building. Say which of the five questions your current provider stumbled on. That is enough for Kevantic to tell you whether Silver identity watching, Gold containment and vulnerability work, or Platinum network detection is the honest starting tier.

Be wary of a proposal that leads with a tool logo and cannot say where logs live, who is awake, or what proves a computer was actually isolated. Be equally wary of a promise that ransomware or phishing will never happen. A serious provider describes the jobs, the record, and the human case. The rest is theatre.
